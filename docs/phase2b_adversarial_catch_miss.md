# Phase 2b Adversarial Characterization: dP_0 Catch/Miss Map

**Status:** research finding. Sedimented from
`tests/test_dp0_adversarial.py` (commit `826af3f`). Companion to the
duality spec in [`phase2b_dp0_magnetic.md`](phase2b_dp0_magnetic.md).

**Purpose:** specify what the verifier asserts and what it does *not*
assert on the dP_0 Seiberg duality fixture. The "miss" rows are equally important as the "catch" rows — they bound the verifier's claim.

## 1. Setup

The base claim is the cross-theory dP_0 duality from Phase 2b-β:
- Electric: 3-node SU(3)³ cyclic toric quiver, 9 bifundamentals,
  W_el = ε_{abc} X^a_AB X^b_BC X^c_CA.
- Magnetic: SU(6)×SU(3)×SU(3) with 12 bifundamentals (q̃, q, M) and
  the 9-monomial effective W_mag described in
  [`phase2b_dp0_magnetic.md`](phase2b_dp0_magnetic.md).

This claim CERTIFIES at default `max_length=6`, `require_r_graded=True`
through `evaluate_claim` with all four upstream anomaly obligations
CERTIFIED and the bounded chiral-ring check passing at every
(length, R) block (Phase 2b-β regression).

Each "adversarial" below modifies the magnetic side in one controlled
way and observes which obligation fires (or doesn't). All tests run
at `max_length=3` for speed, except where noted.

## 3. Catches in detail

### 3.1 Drop-W catches (rows 1, 2)

Dropping any W monomial removes its contribution from three cyclic
derivatives ∂_X W (one for each arrow X appearing in the monomial).
The F-ideal — the two-sided ideal generated by {∂_X W} — therefore
changes.

The *direction* of the dim shift depends on what part of the
symmetric coupling structure is broken:

- **Diagonal drop** (row 1, dim 10 → 14): `M^{(0,0)} q̃[0] q[0]` is
  the *unique* monomial in W coupling `M^{(0,0)}` to a q̃·q pair.
  Dropping it zeroes the generator ∂_{X21[0]} W entirely. The F-ideal
  shrinks; **more** cyclic words survive; magnetic dim *increases*.

- **Off-diagonal drop** (row 2, dim 10 → **4**): `M^{(0,1)}` couples
  symmetrically: W contains both `M^{(0,1)} q̃[0] q[1]` and
  `M^{(0,1)} q̃[1] q[0]`. Dropping one of the two does *not* zero
  the generator; it replaces the symmetric ∂_{X21[1]} W ∝ (X02[0]
  X10[1] + X02[1] X10[0]) with the non-symmetric ∂_{X21[1]} W ∝
  X02[1] X10[0] alone. The new generator is a *more restrictive*
  F-relation than the symmetric sum — it kills the single product
  rather than just the sum. The F-ideal therefore *expands*;
  **fewer** cyclic words survive; magnetic dim *decreases*.

This direction-flip is the headline non-trivial finding of the
characterization. Naïve intuition ("drop = weaker ideal = bigger
dim") is wrong for symmetric-coupling targets.

### 3.2 Non-redundant fake-add catch (row 3)

Adding a new R = 2 closed-walk W monomial introduces a new F-relation.
Whether this changes the quotient dim depends on whether the new
relation is independent of the existing F-ideal at the length being
probed.

`M^{(0,0)} q̃[1] q[2]` is *not* in the symmetric-coupling structure
(M^{(0,0)} should only couple to q̃[0] q[0] in the original W).
The new relation it induces is independent of the existing ideal,
which therefore expands; magnetic dim drops 10 → 7.

(Contrast with row 4 below: a *redundant* R = 2 add is invisible.)

### 3.3 Upstream catch on R-charge perturbation (row 7)

The magnetic R-charges are **uniquely determined** by anomaly
cancellation + W R-consistency:

- Node A mixed anomaly: R(q̃) + R(q) = 2/3
- Node B mixed anomaly: R(q) + R(M) = 5/3
- Node C mixed anomaly: R(q̃) + R(M) = 5/3
- P3: R(q̃) + R(q) + R(M) = 2

Solving: R(q) = R(q̃) = 1/3, R(M) = 4/3 — no degrees of freedom.

Any change to a single R-charge therefore necessarily breaks at
least one constraint. Specifically R(q) = 1/4 breaks both:
- Magnetic mixed anomaly FAILS at node A and node B.
- Magnetic W has R = 1/4 + 1/3 + 4/3 = 23/12 ≠ 2, so P3 FAILS.

Under `require_r_graded = true` either failure suffices to suppress
the bounded chiral-ring check (NOT_APPLICABLE with `P4` listed in
`r_graded_blocked_by`). The R-charge perturbation is caught
*upstream*, not by the chiral-ring layer.

This is the *layered defense* that Phase 2a step 4 was designed
around: P3/P4 catch R-charge and anomaly perturbations; bounded
chiral-ring catches F-ideal perturbations that survive those.

## 4. Misses in detail

### 4.1 Provable miss: overall W rescale (row 5)

This is the only miss with a mathematical proof.

**Claim:** For any non-zero c, the bounded chiral-ring check returns
the same verdict on (electric, magnetic with W → c·W) as on
(electric, magnetic). It necessarily cannot detect overall rescaling.

**Proof sketch:** The F-ideal is the two-sided ideal of the path
algebra generated by the set `{∂_X W : X is an arrow}`. Cyclic
derivative is linear in W, so `∂_X (cW) = c · ∂_X W`. The ideal
generated by `{c · ∂_X W}` (with c ≠ 0) equals the ideal generated
by `{∂_X W}` — multiplication by a non-zero scalar does not change
a linear span. The chiral-ring quotient at every length is therefore
identical, hence every block dim is unchanged.

Tested over three scales `c ∈ {2, −1, 7/3}` to rule out spurious
sign or rationality edge cases. All produce CERTIFIED with dim
10 = 10. **Pinned as a fundamental property of the F-ideal
construction.**

### 4.2 Fixture-specific miss: single-coefficient change (row 6)

Multiplying *just one* W term by a scalar does *not* in general
preserve the F-ideal — single coefficient changes do not commute
with the ideal-generation operation the way overall rescaling does.

On dP_0 magnetic specifically, the perturbation `W[0] → 2 · W[0]`
happens to leave the F-ideal invariant because the perturbed-vs-original
generator delta lies inside the original ideal. This is an
*algebraic coincidence* tied to the symmetric structure of the
magnetic theory, not a generic property of the verifier.

Pinned as CERTIFIED here so the regression catches any algorithm
change that would surface the perturbation, but the docstring (and
this section) are explicit that the result is dP_0-specific.

### 4.3 Fixture-specific miss: redundant R=2 add (row 4)

`M^{(0,0)} q̃[1] q[1]` is a valid R = 2 closed walk, but the
F-relation it induces is already in the original F-ideal (provable
by checking the cyclic-derivative generators at low length on the
relevant arrows). Adding it therefore does not enlarge the ideal,
and the chiral-ring quotient is unchanged.

Sharpens the row-3 catch by demonstrating the boundary: not every
R = 2 closed-walk add is detectable; only the ones whose induced
F-relation is independent of the existing ideal.

### 4.4 Length-only miss: R-charge perturbation under r_graded=False (row 8)

The F-ideal depends on the *coefficients* of W, not on R-charges.
With `require_r_graded = false` the bounded chiral-ring check
ignores R-grading entirely: it computes per-length quotient
dimensions only.

Under that mode, the R(q) = 1/4 perturbation leaves the F-ideal
unchanged (W coefficients unchanged), and the per-length dim 10 =
10 is preserved. CERTIFIED.

This is the bounded chiral-ring layer's *strict* limitation in
length-only mode: it cannot detect R-charge perturbations on its
own. Length-only fallback's CERTIFIED verdict carries no R-symmetry
or anomaly guarantee. **The full physical assertion requires
`require_r_graded = true` *and* the upstream P3/P4 obligations to
have CERTIFIED.**

The verifier records this explicitly: row-8 details include
`r_graded = false` and (per the step-3 fixup) `r_graded_blocked_by`
listing the upstream failures that forced fallback.
